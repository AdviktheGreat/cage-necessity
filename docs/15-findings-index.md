# 15 — Full findings index (all twenty-one)

Every finding this project produced, in rough order of importance, including the exploratory arms that predate the reframe toward the lab's actual questions. The [README](../README.md) now carries only the findings that answer what was asked; this file is the complete record so nothing is lost.

**Findings 1-11 predate the reframe** and several are superseded or retracted; read them as history. **Findings 12-18** answer the mechanistic and cross-model questions. **Findings 19-21** come from the final arm and the specificity re-analysis.
> **Reading the numbers below.** Every "% of CAGE target" in this README is a **peak height** in a
> 10-bin window at the TSS. The source deck's slide 50 implies the objective is an **area** over
> ~160 bp. The choice matters: the lab's `final` is at 55% of target as a peak and **112% as an
> area**. See [correction 11](08-corrections.md). Area readouts are in
> [table10](../data/tables/table10_multitrack_arms.csv).

| # | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **CAGE is necessary in the design loss and nothing substitutes for it.** RNA-only reaches 1.3% of the CAGE target; CAGE+RNA reaches 100%. Substituting DNase (1.4%), Ser5-P Pol II (0.4%), chromatin RNA (12.1%) or **six chromatin marks jointly (1.5%)** all fail. | [Ablations](06-objective-ablations.md), [multi-track](10-multitrack-chromatin.md) |
| 2 | **But CAGE is free at design time.** Borzoi predicts all 7,611 tracks in one forward pass, so a CAGE term costs nothing. CAGE only costs money if you *measure* it. The two questions had been conflated. | [Conclusions](07-conclusions.md) |
| 3 | **No chromatin assay substitutes for CAGE in validation either.** Within one design family CAGE and DNase co-collapse (Pearson +0.942 to +0.998), but across designs from different objectives that drops to +0.218, and among CAGE-containing designs it is **−0.212**. An ATAC-only screen misreads **7 of 21** gene-design combinations, including all three genes of the best design. | [Track coupling](04-context-robustness.md#track-coupling-can-a-chromatin-assay-stand-in-for-cage) |
| 4 | **Synthetic flanking padding is pathological; real genomic padding is fine.** The same design scores **437× differently** in composition-matched synthetic padding versus the real AAVS1 locus — but across **ten random real genomic loci the spread is only 1.55×**, with AAVS1 at the median. The effect is real DNA versus synthetic DNA, not integration site versus elsewhere. | [Context](04-context-robustness.md), [loci](10-multitrack-chromatin.md#6-random-genomic-loci-are-safe-synthetic-random-padding-is-not) |
| 5 | **DNase is essentially not optimizable.** The arm that put DNase *in* the loss achieved the **lowest** DNase of any designed arm (0.100), below the arm that never mentioned it (0.408). | [Ablations](06-objective-ablations.md) |
| 6 | **Same element, different nucleotides.** Scrambling a designed promoter destroys CAGE and DNase together ($r \approx 0.99$), but per-base ISM correlation between them is only −0.04 to +0.35. This explains why DNase is a good *readout* but a bad *objective*. | [ISM](05-ism-mechanism.md) |
| 7 | **Hitting the CAGE target is not the same as building a promoter.** Scored on H3K4me3/H3K4me1, the lab's `final` is **3.60** and every 600-iteration arm here is **≤0.91** — enhancer-like. The arm with the best CAGE attainment (100% of target) has nearly the worst promoter character. The lab's larger edit count is what promoter identity costs. | [Promoter identity](10-multitrack-chromatin.md#4-promoter-identity-final-is-the-only-design-that-has-it), [correction 9](08-corrections.md) |
| 8 | **An independent model confirms the designed sequences carry real initiation syntax.** Scored against ProCapNet's K562 PRO-cap motifs — different model, data and authors — CAGE attainment correlates with initiation-motif content at **Spearman +0.850**. Every CAGE-free arm sits at the 137-motif baseline; every CAGE-containing arm is above it. | [Cross-model check](10-multitrack-chromatin.md#5-cross-model-check-procapnets-motifs-independent-of-borzoi) |
| 9 | **Added constraints make the optimizer abandon a gene rather than degrade all three.** Only **3 of 12** objectives keep all three genes above 10% of their own CAGE target. The failures use *more* edits, so it is an active trade, not under-convergence. A per-gene floor would close it. | [Gene abandonment](10-multitrack-chromatin.md#3-added-constraints-make-the-optimiser-abandon-a-gene) |
| 10 | **A per-gene floor is the single most valuable change to the objective, and its absence invalidated five earlier negative results.** Every added term had been paid for by abandoning a gene. With a 60%-of-target hinge per gene, reverse-strand suppression goes from losing TagBFP at 72.9% CAGE to keeping all three at **114.1%** with the best directionality here (0.915), and H3K4me1 suppression goes from 0.78 to **10.07** promoter character against the lab's 3.60. The floor alone more than doubles promoter character (0.41 → 0.90). | [docs/10 §9](10-multitrack-chromatin.md), [correction 15](08-corrections.md) |
| 11 | ~~**CAGE constrains initiation *direction*.** Orientation index: 8 CAGE-free objectives 0.578–0.760, 9 CAGE-containing 0.838–0.993, no overlap, lab's `final` 0.993.~~ **RETRACTED (correction 18).** The index summed 5 bins running *downstream* of the TSS while divergent initiation peaks 25–250 bp *upstream*, so the window was blind to the phenomenon by construction. The lab's `final` scored 0.99 here while running more antisense than sense under one PRO-cap model. Direction is still the right discriminator and PRO-cap measures it natively at 1 bp — but this evidence for it does not stand. See findings 13–14. | [corr 18](08-corrections.md) |
| 12 | **What CAGE is doing is installing CpG islands, and that is why accessibility follows.** CpG count is the best sequence predictor of DNase (Spearman +0.608, beating every motif; TATA is *negative*). Objectives with CAGE add +8.4 CpGs per promoter window vs +4.0 without; the lab's `final` adds +17. **Holding CpG fixed, CAGE explains almost nothing about accessibility (+0.102); holding CAGE fixed, CpG still explains a lot (+0.429).** Asking for DNase directly fails because it adds only 2 CpGs. **Tested directly and the substitution fails** (correction 17): a CpG constraint with no CAGE hit the target count at all three promoters but produced a DNase peak at only one of them, and no initiation. It did get eGFP to 3.16 against the lab's 3.70, where no previous CAGE-free arm exceeded 0.86, so accessibility does not *require* CAGE. Accessibility and initiation are two requirements and only the first has a free proxy. | [docs/11](11-what-cage-is-doing.md) |
| 13 | **Whether the designed promoters initiate divergently like enhancers is model-dependent, and only one of them fails under both models.** ProCapNet scores the lab's `final` as divergent at all three promoters (1.7–3.7× more antisense than sense); AlphaGenome passes two of the three (0.108, 0.236) and fails only eGFP (1.233). So **0 of 3 becomes 2 of 3** depending on the judge (correction 20) — I sent the harsher version to the authors before checking it against a second model. What survives both is the **eGFP promoter specifically**, which carries a large antisense peak 270 bp upstream. This still retracts the orientation index (correction 18), which was measured over 5 bins running *downstream* of the TSS while divergent initiation peaks 25–250 bp *upstream*. | [docs/12](12-procap-crossmodel.md), [docs/13 §5](13-alphagenome-arbitration.md) |
| 14 | **The durable benefit of designing against PRO-cap is positional precision, not accessibility and not promoter count.** RNA + PRO-cap places the initiation peak at **−4 / +17 / +0 bp** from the three designed start sites, where 15 CAGE-designed promoters scatter from **−172 to +148 bp** and the lab's `final` sits at +41 / +27 / +46. Real promoters land at −1. But the claim that this gave real promoters at all three genes was **overfitting to the oracle in the loop** (correction 20): ProCapNet, which was being optimised, scores 3 of 3; AlphaGenome scores **1 of 3**. Accessibility does not follow either, at 2.43 / 0.18 / 0.10 by Borzoi and 4.34 / 0.42 / 0.23 by AlphaGenome — two independent models agreeing it failed. | [docs/13 §4, §6](13-alphagenome-arbitration.md) |
| 15 | **The deliverable itself — three genes at 7/5/3 — does not survive an independent model.** Borzoi, which had RNA in its loss, says the ladder is in the right order for **7 of 7** designs. **AlphaGenome 1 and AlphaGenome 2, neither in any design loop, both say 3 of 7 and return identical verdicts on all seven designs.** For the lab's `final`, Borzoi gives 7.76 / 7.09 / 4.89 and AlphaGenome gives **0.050 / 0.270 / 1.820 — inverted**, with TagBFP at 37× eGFP where the target is 0.43×. `final`'s profile also stays high past the last gene (0.631 vs 1.820), a read-through pattern rather than three independent genes; the premature `AATAAA` 34 bp after the mScarlet stop is a candidate cause and Borzoi is blind to it. AG2, released 2026-11-11 and trained on >100 M single-cell profiles, replicates it; the two versions differ on severity (AG1 a 23x inverted spread, AG2 a 2x one against a 2.33x target), so the dynamic range is about right and the order is wrong. Only order and ratio are comparable, and nothing here says which model is right — but the project's actual output has only ever been confirmed by the model optimised to produce it. | [docs/13 §7](13-alphagenome-arbitration.md) |
| 16 | **A readout that was in the loss is a diagnostic, not evidence — and this is measurable.** Cross-model agreement in this project falls monotonically with how hard the objective pushed on the readout: DNase, never optimised, Spearman **+0.899**; PRO-cap, optimised in one arm, +0.648; CAGE, optimised in most arms, **+0.516**; the RNA ladder, optimised in every arm, reproduces in **2 of 7**. The same assay across two models agrees *worse* than two different assays within one model (AlphaGenome CAGE vs its own PRO-cap: **+0.953**). This also retracts the headline that the two initiation assays fundamentally disagree (correction 19) — that was Borzoi versus ProCapNet, not CAGE versus PRO-cap. The conclusion that external filtering is load-bearing survives; the stated reason was wrong. | [docs/13 §3, §8](13-alphagenome-arbitration.md) |
| 17 | **Borzoi's DNase predictions are confirmed by an independent model, so the accessibility conclusions stand.** Borzoi DNase vs AlphaGenome DNase across 21 design-gene pairs: Pearson **+0.872**, Spearman **+0.899**, including on the PRO-cap-designed locus Borzoi had never been optimised for. This closes the open caveat from docs/12 §6 — Borzoi was not blind to that sequence, it was right about it. Accessibility remains the one thing in this project that two unrelated models agree on, and it is the one readout no objective ever optimised. | [docs/13 §2](13-alphagenome-arbitration.md) |
| 18 | **AlphaGenome 2 cannot replace AlphaGenome 1 here, and neither can design.** AG2 (released 2026-11-11) **removes PRO-cap entirely**, so every directionality result in this repo has to stay on AG1 and ProCapNet. Its new input-gradient endpoint is keyed to an annotated `gene_id`, not an arbitrary sequence, so it cannot be pointed at a synthetic construct; with no released weights in either version, AlphaGenome is a filter and never a design oracle. What AG2 does add is a stronger expression model (which replicates finding 15), 385 DNase tracks against 305, scATAC, and **methylation** — and that last one clears a worry raised by finding 12: the designed CpG islands are predicted **unmethylated** (0.013–0.024) in the same range as real housekeeping promoters (0.006–0.015), so they are not intrinsic methylation targets. That says nothing about silencing of an integrated transgene, which no sequence model sees. | [docs/13 §10](13-alphagenome-arbitration.md) |
| 19 | ~~**The dual-oracle arm is the best design on DNase at promoters.**~~ **RETRACTED (correction 22).** The metric was absolute DNase height at the weakest promoter, which a design can satisfy by opening the *whole locus* rather than building three promoters. Measured against untouched background, the dual arm is 12.5x/19.3x specific while the lab's `final` is **41.8x/36.3x**. What survives: the arm does achieve the highest absolute accessibility (4.59/5.74) and abandons no gene (CAGE 100.5/119.2/92.7%, ProCapNet reads 46.3/82.7/99.3), so the per-gene floors worked. The recommendation changes from this arm to `B_cage_rna_floor`. | [corr 22](08-corrections.md), [docs/14](14-dual-oracle.md) |
| 20 | **Adding a second independent oracle to the loss does not fix oracle overfitting; both oracles get fooled together.** This was a direct prospective test of finding 16, and the hypothesis failed. With ProCapNet *and* Borzoi both in the objective, ProCapNet calls 2 of 3 promoters divergent and AlphaGenome 1, in no loss, calls **0 of 3** (antisense/sense 0.308 / 0.049 / 0.149 versus 3.295 / 1.215 / 0.475). The ranking inverts completely: the design that looks **most** divergent to the independent model is the lab's `final`, at 2 of 3, which never saw a PRO-cap track, while the two arms explicitly optimised for divergence come last. So finding 16 is not just a correlation across arms — it reproduces on demand. **The fix for oracle overfitting is not a better loss or more oracles in it; it is holding a model back.** Filter on readouts absent from the loss. | [docs/14](14-dual-oracle.md) |
| 21 | **"DNase peaks at promoters" needs two axes, and the lab's own design wins the one they named.** Peak height and locality are different things. Against background on the 67% of the construct no objective touched, the lab's `final` reaches **41.8x** (AG1) and **36.3x** (AG2), against 15.2x/33.8x for CAGE+RNA+floor and 12.5x/19.3x for the dual-oracle arm; `final` is top in **7 of 9** model x window combinations. The source deck's Figure 4 already drew exactly this distinction ("runaway, broad peaks, mostly off-target" versus "peaks **at** the designed positions"), so this is an independent confirmation of the lab's own claim by two models with no part in the design. No design wins both axes; the Pareto front is `final` (most local), `B_cage_rna_floor` (balanced, best by AG2) and the dual arm (strongest). | [README §1](../README.md#1-the-primary-target-dnase-peaks-at-promoters), [table16](../data/tables/table16_promoter_specificity.csv) |
